# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person on the other end is acting in good faith, is busy, and
knows things you do not.

## What is expected

Keep discussion on the technical substance. Disagree with the argument rather than the person.
Accept that the maintainer may decline a change, and that a declined change is not a judgement
about you. If you are reporting a problem, say what you observed and what you expected; if you
are responding to a report, take it seriously even when it turns out to be a misunderstanding.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory or demeaning remarks, deliberate intimidation,
sustained disruption, publishing someone's private information, and unwelcome attention of a
sexual nature. This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review and any
other project space, and it applies regardless of who you are or how useful your contributions
have been.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through GitHub. Reports will be read and handled
discreetly, and the reporter's identity will not be shared beyond what is needed to act.

The maintainer may warn, edit or remove content, or block an account from the project, at their
discretion and without a formal appeals process. This is a one-person project, so the process
is informal by necessity, not an invitation to test its limits.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces, and also when someone is representing the project in a
public space.

This document is deliberately short and original to this project. If you want the thorough,
widely adopted version of the same idea, read the Contributor Covenant at
https://www.contributor-covenant.org/.
